    <h2 class="subhead">The highlights</h2>
    <h3>BRG Kings Island — the island classic</h3>
    <p>Vietnam's northern golf pioneer. Three distinct layouts (55 holes) sit on an island in Dong Mo lake, reached by speedboat. The Lakeside course dates to 1993; the Kings Course is a modern Nicklaus II design. Allow half a day — the boat transfer and three-course choice make it worth the trip.</p>
    <h3>Sky Lake — best value resort</h3>
    <p>36 holes in two contrasting layouts on the western edge of Hanoi, backed by Wyndham Sky Lake resort facilities. The most consistent option for a comfortable, reasonably priced golf day.</p>
    <h3>Long Bien — closest to the city</h3>
    <p>27 holes of parkland on the Red River, 15–20 minutes from downtown. Ideal for a half day, a business round, or an arrival-day warm-up before you head out to the bigger resorts.</p>
    <h3>Stone Highland — new and spectacular</h3>
    <p>A Brian Curley mountain course on unusual rose-gold rock terrain, an hour-and-a-half from Hanoi. Photogenic, hilly and genuinely different from everything else in the region.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="prose">
    <h2 class="subhead">Planning tips for a Hanoi golf trip</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Best season:</strong> October to April brings mild, dry weather. Summer rounds start early to avoid midday heat and thunderstorms.</li>
      <li><strong>Getting around:</strong> Use a private car or arrange a tour package — course-to-course distances are 30–90 minutes, and taxis are impractical.</li>
      <li><strong>Weekends:</strong> Course supply near Hanoi is tight on Saturdays. Book tee times at least 3–7 days ahead.</li>
      <li><strong>Combine trips:</strong> Pair a Ninh Binh day with Legend Valley and the karst scenery of Trang An — two rounds over two days with a night in the province.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
